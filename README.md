# Gunslinger

Gunslinger is a first-person browser combat game with two maps, picked on the title screen:

- **Harbor** — a rain-swept container terminal, featuring swept projectile ballistics, a live 6-weapon modular gunsmith system, procedural WebAudio synthesis, and a physically grounded WebGL 2 lighting and reflection pipeline. Modes: Mission (cut three uplinks and extract) and Holdout.
- **Churchyard** — a ruined church, its graveyard and the pine forest around it, at night in the rain. Mode: Crystal Vigil, a wave defense against the dead for one to four players.

Run `npm start`, then open http://localhost:8080. It needs Node 18 or newer (for the small local server and the tests) and a desktop browser with WebGL 2. No package install or build step is required. Google Fonts is optional; system fallbacks work offline.

On the title screen, **Operator** opens the thirty outfits your soldier can wear and the challenges that earn them, and **Leaderboards** shows the best runs on every map and mode; both are described under [Operator, challenges and leaderboards](#operator-challenges-and-leaderboards).

Changing the map on the title screen reloads the page, because a map is a whole world: level, sky, ground and enemies. The choice is remembered, and `?map=harbor` or `?map=vigil` in the address selects one directly. **Main menu** on the pause, defeat and completion cards goes back to the title screen.

The next three sections describe the Harbor; the Churchyard has its own rules and keys, listed under it.

## Controls & Tactical Systems

- **Movement & Stance**: **WASD** move, mouse look, **Shift** sprint, **C / Ctrl** crouch, **R** dashes on the Churchyard and **X** on the Harbor (a short lunge the way you are moving, or the way you face standing still; it works in the air, and can be done again after about a second), **E** interact, **Esc** pause.
- **Weapons & Fire Control**: **1–6** or mouse wheel selects across six modular weapons; **Q** swaps to the previous weapon. **LMB** fires, **RMB** aims down sights, **R** reloads, **V** switches semi / auto on select-fire rifles, **F** performs a quick melee strike, and **I** inspects the held weapon.
- **Live Modular Gunsmith (`Tab` / `H`)**: Opens the in-field CAD workbench to hot-swap optics, muzzle devices, barrels, underbarrel grips/lasers, ammunition/magazines, and Cerakote/PVD finishes on any of the six platforms with live stat bars and 3D hardware updates. Click pills or use **↑/↓** and **←/→** (`Enter` / `Tab` to return to combat, `Factory` to reset the held build).
- **Tactical Emitter (`T`)**: Cycles the 3-position rotary mode on mounted AN/PEQ-15 and X300V units (`VIS LASER` → `DUAL LASER + LIGHT` → `OFF`), projecting a raycast visible green laser beam/dot and high-CRI weaponlight cone while tightening hip-fire stability.
- **Ballistics & Combat Physiology**:
  - Swept projectiles simulate travel time, gravity, range limits, damage falloff, supersonic tracers, and acoustic bullet-snap suppression. Cover stops rounds; armor penetration (`Tungsten AP`) increases weak-point lethality on armored machines, while world-cover penetration and ricochet are not yet simulated.
  - Health does not regenerate. Hold **B** while stationary for three seconds to apply one of two field dressings (+35 health); incoming damage interrupts treatment, and critical injuries slow walking.
  - Sprinting drains endurance with exhaustion hysteresis; holding **Alt** while aiming steadies breath for a bounded window.
- **Controller.** Both maps play on a controller: sticks move and look, **RT** fires, **LT** aims, **A** jumps, **B** crouches, **X** reloads on the Harbor (and cuts a relay when one is in reach), where the dash is **LB + X**; on the Churchyard **X** is the dash, and beside the ammunition pile it takes ammunition instead, **Y** swaps rifle and sidearm, the left stick's click sprints (and steadies the aim while aiming), the right stick's click is melee, **D-pad left / right** steps through the weapons carried, **Menu** pauses. The Harbor adds **View** for the gunsmith, **RB** fire mode, **LB** inspect, **D-pad up** laser and light, and **D-pad down** held for a dressing. The Churchyard adds **View** for the armory, **LB** the class ability, **RB** a grenade, **D-pad up** the flashlight, **D-pad down** a medkit, and the rest as combinations held with **LB**: **X** flare, **Y** mine, **B** shockwave, **A** aegis, **D-pad up** call a wave, **D-pad left** laser, **D-pad down** inspect, **D-pad right** change pistol, **View** gunsmith. A button that begins a combination does its own job when you let it go. In the armory, the gunsmith, Operator, Leaderboards, Skills and the binding table the D-pad or left stick moves a highlight, **A** presses, **B** goes back and **LB / RB** change tab; with a defense in hand **RT** sets it down and **LT** puts it back. The HUD and the armory name whichever key or button is bound, for whichever is in the hand.
- **Your name.** The title screen always has a Name box. It is what a squad sees and what the leaderboards call you (in capitals: A to Z, 0 to 9, space, and - _ .; sixteen characters). Change it any time. Rows of players who have not typed one still read "Warden" and four characters of their id.
- **Rebinding.** Settings, Controls: every action has two keyboard slots and one controller slot. Choose one and press the key or button (**Esc** cancels, **Backspace** clears; the mouse's side buttons count as keys). For a controller combination hold one button and press another. A key or button taken for one action is given up by the one that had it. There are separate resets for keyboard and controller, a controller sensitivity and inversion of its own, and a vibration switch. Weapon numbers, **Esc**, the mouse's three buttons and the wheel are fixed. Saved in the browser (`gunslinger.controls.v1`); the actions both maps share are bound once.
  - Touch covers the engine's base actions only.

Cut three uplinks and extract, or choose the inherited holdout mode. Relay B sits in the transit shed, Relay C on the quay, and Relay A in the South Stack Yard plaza, so a full operation crosses the gate, yard, stack yard, shed and quay. Difficulty changes preserve the custom movement and non-regenerating health rules.

## Terminal Layout

The playable Berth 4 terminal is 78 m × 114 m (x −48…30 inland, quay to the sea at x 30; z −38…76), laid out like a working container terminal: blocks of standard 20 ft / 40 ft boxes in bays, straddle lanes between them, regularly spaced light masts, and the reefer row next to its power.

- **Gate & yard** (west and centre): the original approach, yard stacks and the stairs-and-catwalk vantage at z ≈ 15.
- **Transit shed** (north): interior combat around Relay B.
- **Quay** (east): crane rails, bollards and the extraction boat station, now running the full length of the terminal.
- **South Stack Yard** (new, z 40…76): a service road at z ≈ 41 opens onto a north bay row with three entrances; a 3-tier **west canyon** (a 3.8 m alley between two rows of 40 ft stacks) with a narrow flank path along the fence; the **Relay A plaza** with mid-height cover; a second **stairs → container → catwalk → container overwatch** on the east side; a powered **reefer row** along the south fence with a rear flank corridor behind it; and reefer stacks and cover on the extended quay. Enemy ground/air spawns and AI cover points cover the new block.

## The Churchyard: Crystal Vigil

You defend a crystal on the dais of a ruined church. The church has four ways in, one on each side: the main door to the south, a side door in the west wall and another in the east, and a breach in the north wall. The dead come out of the forest all round it, through the four gates and eight collapsed stretches of the graveyard wall; from wave 2 some claw their way up through graves and open ground, and some walk out of the four mausoleums. You lose when the crystal breaks.

- **Classes** (picked on the title screen). Soldier: KR-7 Carbine, +15% damage, Adrenaline. Medic: KR-9 PDW, health regeneration, Sanctify. Engineer: KR-12 Shotgun (the arsenal's Breacher, a 12-gauge semi-automatic), cheaper and stronger defenses, a free turret, Overclock. Scout: KR-7 Marksman, faster, stronger headshots, Flare Shot.
- **Guns and ammunition.** A night starts with the PAC-4 pistol and nothing else. It never runs dry. Every other gun is bought in the armory, the carbine included (PDW $1,060, KR-12 $1,630, pump $2,250, carbine $2,630, marksman $3,630, support $6,500, flak cannon $9,000, rocket launcher $14,000), and everything bought is gone when the night is: a new night, or one begun at a wave of five, starts with the pistol again. Only Continue keeps it. Nothing is reloaded. A gun fires from what you carry of the kind of round it takes, and guns of a kind share it: 9 mm (the PDW; 240 carried), rifle rounds (carbine, marksman, support; 300), shells (both shotguns; 48), flak shells (16) and rockets (6). A gun comes with some of its kind. What matters is how much you can carry (Deep Pouches, the Bandolier perk) and how fast you fire (Light Trigger, Quick Hands, Adrenaline).
- **The ammunition pile.** By the crates and the cask on the east wall of the nave there is a heap of ammunition crates, bins of loose rounds and rockets under a board that reads BUY AMMO HERE! Stand by it and the screen says what filling up would cost; **E** (a controller's **X**) buys it. You pay for what you are short of and nothing more: a full fill from empty is about $120 for 9 mm, $225 for rifle rounds, $150 for shells, $440 for flak and $490 for rockets, and half as much short costs half. With too little money you get the same share of each kind for what you have. Every visit that buys anything makes the next one $20 dearer for the rest of the night, on top of the rounds: the first is the rounds alone, the second $20 more, the third $40 more. The pile cannot be destroyed. The armory no longer sells ammunition.
- **Down, and back.** With a respawn left you are back on your feet in four seconds, outside at the gate where the night began. While you are down the camera circles the crystal, close, inside the church. A night that is lost begins again at the last wave of five it reached (lost at wave 13: from wave 10), with the pistol and a first wave's money.
- **Reading a fight.** Every hit you land shows what it did as a number rising off the body (a shotgun's pellets added up, head shots in yellow). While an abomination is in the yard its health is a bar across the top of the screen, for every player; two abominations, two bars.
- **Blood.** Every one of the dead bleeds when it is hit, more through the head, and most when it dies: a burst off the side it was shot from, a spray out of the far side, a fountain from a neck, a stump that goes on pumping and leaves a trail while the thing still walks, and everything at once when one is blown apart. Where it lands it stays for the rest of the night: on the floor beyond and under the body, running down the wall behind it, and as a pool that spreads under a body once it lies still. There is room for 6,000 marks (after that the oldest is painted over), and no more than four begin in the same half metre of a surface: after that the ones there grow. A mark on a wall or a barricade goes when that comes down. Settings has **Blood** (on or off) and **Blood colour**: six colours, one of your own, or Rainbow, a different colour for every wound. With it off the dead give off dust.
- **Repairs cost what is broken.** Crystal Repair mends the whole crystal, and the whole crystal is worth $3,000 before the armory's mark-up: a quarter of it gone is a quarter of that. Repair Defenses costs 60% of what each guard ($900) or turret ($1,200) cost new, for the share of it that is gone: a guard at half health and a turret all but destroyed came to $1,150 at the armory's prices. With less money than the price, what you have buys its share of the mending.
- **What breaks goes off.** Anything destroyed goes with a burst of fire that hurts whoever stands beside it, you included: a crate is a slap (up to 18 at arm's length, out to 2 m), a bay of the church wall is not to be stood under (up to 60, out to about 5 m). It kills the dead beside it. It does not set off the next thing, and what falls afterwards hurts nobody. The pieces lie for 15 seconds from the break, then sink into the ground and are gone; severed limbs and bodies do the same. What is left standing of a thing stays.
- **The altar.** You jump 1.35 m here, enough to get onto the altar from its step. The altar, its cross, candlesticks and book, the screen behind it and both candle stands can be destroyed; what stands on the altar comes down with it, and a candle goes out with its holder (the chandeliers' too).
- **Money.** Every kill pays, headshots pay more, and clearing a wave pays a bonus. Kills by guards and turrets pay 60%. What a kill paid rises off the body where it fell.
- **Armory (B).** Every gun but the first pistol; which rifle and sidearm are in hand; grenades, flares, medkits, mines, armor, crystal repair and respawns; ability upgrades and passive perks; guards, turrets and floodlights. The fight pauses while it is open. Defenses are set down after you close it: aim, left click to place, right click to cancel for a refund.
- **Continue.** The night is saved between waves: when a wave is cleared, while you prepare for the next, and as it begins. Leave, close the tab or reload, and the title screen offers **Continue** with the wave, class, money and crystal it will resume from: you start inside the church, before the wave you were on, with the weapons, ammunition, items, perks, lifeforce skills, defenses and broken buildings you had then. A wave you were in the middle of is fought again from its start. One night is kept, in this browser. When the crystal falls the saved night is thrown away. Beginning a new night leaves a saved one alone until the new night has cleared a wave of its own.
- **Starting further in.** Reach wave 5, 10, 15 and so on, once, and from then on the title screen's **Start** row lets a new night begin at any of those waves. It begins with the money and kit of a first wave: only your Skills carry over. This is kept with your save, so it follows you to another device.
- **Buildings.** The church, its tower and the four mausoleums can be brought down. A wall goes a bay at a time: bullets, grenades and anything else that explodes wear a bay down (about 120 rifle rounds or ten grenades against it), a corner breaks away, then the top comes down in pieces and leaves a stump that still blocks the way; keep at the stump and the bay is gone, and the dead walk through the gap. The roof over a bay falls when a wall under it has gone, a door comes off with its wall, a chandelier with its roof, and each tier of the tower follows the one below it. The dead do not break buildings themselves. Everything stands again when a new night begins.
- **The pump shotgun.** The WS-12 (key 0) is bought in the armory, like the semi-automatic KR-12 (key 5) whose shells it shares. Twelve pellets a shell at 26 each against the KR-12's nine at 20, and it has to be worked between shots: the fore-end comes back and goes forward under your left hand, about one shot a second. It does more with each shell and less in a minute.
- **The flak cannon and the rocket launcher.** Two heavy weapons are bought in the armory, not carried from the start. The FK-40 Flak Cannon ($9,000, key 8) fires 40 mm shells you can watch fly: each bursts where it strikes, or beside the first body it passes within about two metres once it is six metres out, measured in three dimensions, so it works on what flies (250 at the centre, 4.4 m, five in the drum, twenty in reserve). The RL-90 Rocket Launcher ($14,000, key 9) holds one rocket and loads the next by itself in under three seconds: 950 at the centre over 7.5 m, enough for a packed doorway, for a bay of the church's wall in five or six, and for you inside five metres. Both slow you a little and kick hard. Ammo Resupply charges for each missing shell and rocket on top of its own price.
- **The mausoleums.** Each one still standing lets out a wanderer every quarter of a minute or so. They keep to themselves until you come near, hurt one or fire close by, and they are worth money and lifeforce like any other. Knock the top off a mausoleum and it stops.
- **Going down.** You start with one respawn. Without one you watch from above until the wave ends, so the guards and turrets have to hold.
- **The dead.** Walkers, runners, acid-spitters, brutes, and an abomination every fifth wave. Zombies you shoot come after you; the rest go for the crystal.
- **How they come.** Each wave favours a few directions in the woods and sends strays from everywhere else. Each zombie is given one of the four ways into the church, likelier the nearer and the less crowded it is, and walks a line of its own there: they arrive as a spread front, weaving, not as a column, and close on a living target from the sides. One that passes close to another open door takes that one. A brute and the abomination are too big for the paths the others take, so each has its own map of where it fits, cut to its width and height: neither is sent at a gap, a post or a doorway it cannot pass. A brute fits all four ways in; the abomination keeps to the main door and the breach, because a side door's arch is lower than its head.
- **The newer dead.** Three kinds join the walkers, runners, spitters and brutes. A **wolf** (from wave 3, and now and then loose in the woods from wave 2) goes on all fours faster than a runner; with you in sight between four and ten metres off it sinks for a moment and springs, 16 damage if it lands on you. A **burster** (from wave 4) walks up, swells for a second and goes off: 42 damage at its feet falling to about half at five metres, and it throws you back; shot down, it goes off where it stands at half strength and hurts the dead round it, and put down through the head it does not go off at all. A **pyre** (from wave 6) burns as it walks and throws fire from up to 24 metres, 16 for a hit and half that to anyone within 2.6 metres of where it lands. Together they are never more than a fifth of a wave.
- **The abomination's fire.** It throws three bolts of fire in a fan at anyone it can see across the yard. With someone inside seven metres it gathers itself for a second, arms up and the fire drawn in to it, then lets off a ring that burns everything within 8.5 metres (30 at its feet, less at the edge, and the crystal too). And every quarter of a minute or so it walks wreathed in flame for eight seconds: standing within 3.8 metres of it then costs 11 health a second.
- **What is not a person.** From wave 4 a share of each wave that grows to a fifth is not the dead at all. **Spiders** (wave 4) run the ground like the rest, spring like the wolf (14), and with you 4 to 15 metres off make for the nearest wall, climb two or three metres up it and drop on you from there. **Bats** (wave 5) come three at a time, circle overhead and dive through you for 9 a pass. A **demon head** (wave 6) flies in at head height; inside 13 metres it gathers itself for half a second and charges, and goes off where it arrives (34 at its centre, less to 4.4 metres) on you, on a guard or turret, or on the crystal. Shoot it down first and it only bursts. A **demon** (wave 7) hovers three or four metres up, 9 to 13 metres off, drifting round you and throwing fire (13, half that near where it lands). From wave 9 one to three **great spiders** come with the brutes: 620 health, 24 a bite, 30 when they land on you from a wall. Everything that flies stays below the eaves and comes in through the doors when it cannot see you. The flak cannon's shells burst beside what flies.
- **How they move.** The dead walk by motion capture: gait cycles taken from the 100STYLE dataset's Zombie, LimpLeft and DragLeftLeg performances (three shambles, a limp and a dragged leg on either side, two runs for the runners, and a standing pose they settle into when they stop). Each zombie is given one. The game adds what is its own: a longer stride for speeds no shamble covers, a hunch that deepens when it is after someone, arms that each carries its own way (half hold them out as the performer did, the rest lower), the swing of an attack, the flinch from a hit, a slumped chest, a head that tilts and looks about. The hips ride at whatever height stands the lower foot on the ground. Crawling, rising from the earth and the ragdoll are as they were. `?motion=0` brings back the old formula walk for comparison.
- **The wild dead.** Eight to fourteen zombies wander the woods and the graveyard all night, wave or no wave: walkers mostly, some runners and spitters, a brute or two once the waves have begun, and from wave 5 sometimes an abomination far out in the trees. They leave the crystal alone and are not counted in any wave. One wakes if you come within its senses (nine metres for a walker, further for a runner), shoot it, or fire within earshot (27 m for a rifle, 8 m suppressed); from then on it hunts you, and when it loses you it goes for the crystal. They pay like any other kill, so waking them between waves is a wave of your own making.
- **Graves.** Break a headstone and, two times in three, what is buried there claws its way up and comes for you. Each grave opens once a night.
- **Totems.** Left alone, up to three of the wild dead gather at a spot in the woods or a corner of the graveyard and raise a totem of stone and timber, in three stages. A finished one calls another zombie out of the ground near it every 22 seconds, up to four more than the night would otherwise keep, until you shoot it down (about two hits for each stage it has reached; it pays $40 a stage).
- **Being hit.** Red closes in from the edge of the screen, thickest on the side the blow came from, and stays in closer the less health you have; you grunt, and a heartbeat keeps time while you are badly hurt.
- **Music.** A pipe organ alone between waves and on the title screen. When a wave is called, organ and heavy guitars come up from behind a closed filter over the first bars. A faster cue takes over when waves overlap, when the yard is full, when the crystal is below a third, and for the whole of a wave that brings an abomination; the abomination has a theme of its own from the moment it appears until it falls. When the last of a wave is down the fight fades out and the organ returns. Gunfire and being hurt dip it, the pause screen quietens it, and it is muffled while you are down. Its level is the Music slider in Settings. There is no key for it in play: `M` once muted it, and sits between the armory's `B` and the mine's `N`.
- **The storm.** Forked lightning comes down beyond the trees: a jagged channel with branches, the clouds lit round it, the churchyard flaring for a moment, and thunder after a wait that is longer the further off it fell. It strikes every few seconds on the title screen, less often in play, more often while an abomination walks, and one answers the abomination's arrival. With the system's "reduce motion" setting on, a strike does not flicker and comes less than half as often.
- **The rain.** Heavy and meant to be seen, on the title screen as in play: long streaks close to the eye, a second layer of heavier ones out to thirty metres that reads as sheets across the yard and against the sky, every drop lit for a moment by lightning, and rain falling through a lantern's glow or the flashlight's beam lit by it. On the title screen the sound of it plays at half level (both maps).
- **Hearing the dead.** Every zombie has a voice and its own time to use it, heard out to about fifty metres. A round going into one makes a bright slap and a wet thump that carries past the sound of the shot; half the time the body answers with a bark of pain. A head shot cracks. One that turns on you snarls first. A body shot dead groans as it goes; one shot through the head does not.
- **Limbs.** Arms and legs have their own health. Enough damage takes one off at the elbow, knee, shoulder or hip, and a grenade or mine can take several. A zombie that loses a leg drops and drags itself on by its arms, slower and lower; one that loses its arms bites; one with neither writhes for a few seconds and dies. Brutes can be crippled the same way. The abomination cannot be brought down by its legs.
- **Bodies.** The dead fall as ragdolls from whatever pose they were in, pushed by what killed them, and lie where they land for a while. A blast throws bodies and severed limbs that are already down.
- **Breaking things.** Gravestones, crosses, urns, the font, pews, chairs, the pulpit, benches, crates and casks break. Stone chips where it is hit, then snaps and leaves a stump low enough to step over; timber splits into lengths. Explosions break what is near them, and brutes smash what stands in their way. Everything is whole again on a new run.
- **Stacking waves.** Enter starts the next wave at once. Pressed during a wave it adds the next one on top, as many times as you like: five presses on wave 1 puts waves 1 to 5 in play together. The round ends when the last zombie of all of them is down, and each extra wave cleared in the same round adds 15% to the bonus.

### Playing together

Crystal Vigil can be played by up to four people at once, through [Multiplayer Agent](https://multiplayeragent.com). On the title screen set **Squad** to **Co-op**: the game joins a room straight away and lists who else is in it. **Copy invite** gives a link that opens the same room on someone else's machine; **New room** makes a private one. Anyone can begin; later arrivals drop into the run in progress with extra cash to catch up.

- The waves, the crystal, the dead, what is broken and what has been placed are shared. Money, kit, perks, items and respawns are each player's own.
- A kill pays whoever made it (a turret's or guard's kill pays whoever placed it); clearing a wave pays everyone. Each extra player adds 60% more zombies to a wave.
- Nothing pauses. In the armory or the pause menu you are out of the fight: the dead ignore you and cannot hurt you, and the wave carries on.
- If the crystal falls, anyone's restart begins a new run for the room.
- The best wave you have cleared goes on a leaderboard as you clear it, shown on the title screen.

Without a key (see `mp.config.js`) or a connection, the Squad row stays on Solo and the game is exactly the single-player one. The Harbor is single-player only.

The Churchyard uses the Harbor's modelled arsenal under its own rules. Each rifle kit is one of the arsenal's rifles: the KR-7 Carbine (3), the suppressed KR-9 PDW (4), the KR-12 Breacher (5), the scoped KR-7 Marksman (6) and the KR-7 Support (7), a carbine with a 100-round twin-drum magazine and a folded bipod that exists only here. ## Operator, challenges and leaderboards

**The soldier.** Your character is a soldier from War Bash, wearing one of its thirty outfits. Other players see it in co-op: the outfit you chose, the rifle shouldered where you aim, walking, crouching, and laid on its back when you are down. The **Operator** screen shows the outfit on a soldier standing in the map itself, under that map's light; drag to turn it. Four outfits are standard issue (Assault, Commando, Ghillie, Breacher). The other twenty-six are earned.

**Challenges.** Each earned outfit has three challenges; finish all three and it is yours to wear. Eleven outfits are earned in the Harbor (reaching the boat, Holdout waves and scores, kills by weapon, range and weak point), eleven in the Churchyard (waves, classes, kits, zombie types, the crystal kept whole), and four anywhere or with a squad. They count while you play, on either map; a line on the left of the screen says when one completes or an outfit unlocks, and the card at the end of a run lists what the run moved. Locked outfits can be looked at, with how far each challenge has come. Progress is kept in the browser and, when the game has a Multiplayer Agent key, in your player's save there, so it follows you to another device once you are the same player on it.

**Levels.** Everything you do earns experience on both maps: a kill (10 for a walker up to 400 for an abomination, 20 for a harbor machine; half as much again through the head or the weak point, half for one a turret or guard made), a wave cleared, a relay cut, the boat reached, a totem felled, a challenge finished, an outfit earned. The curve and the eleven ranks, Recruit to Legend, are War Bash's: level 2 at 250 experience, level 10 at 8,409, level 100 at 389,903. The title screen shows your badge, level and how far to the next; the card at the end of a run shows what the run was worth. Experience is saved with the outfits, in this browser and in your Multiplayer Agent save.

**Skills (Churchyard).** Every level gained is a point. Skills, on the Churchyard's title screen, spends them on eighteen skills in three branches of six: Gunfighter (bullet damage, rate of fire, head-shot damage, ammunition carried, rounds that pass through one more body, more damage to the big ones), Survivor (health, recovering when unhurt, speed, medkits, taking less damage, one more respawn) and Warden (money from kills, money at the start, crystal health, grenades, the crystal's healing between waves, a faster class ability). A skill needs at least one rank of the one above it. Each rank of a skill costs two points more than the one before: 1, then 3, then 5 (the single-rank skills at the foot of each branch cost 2 and 3). All eighteen in full cost 78 points. A tree learned at the old prices that now costs more than your points counts as empty, with every point back to spend. Refund all gives every point back at no cost. What you have learned applies from the start of the next night.

**Lifeforce (Churchyard).** Every zombie that dies, whoever killed it, leaves a wisp of green light over its body: 1 for a walker or a runner, 2 for a spitter, a wolf or a burster, 3 for a pyre, 6 for a brute, 30 for an abomination. Walk near and it comes to you; left alone it is gone after a minute. The count is under the cash. It is spent in the armory (`B`), on the Lifeforce tab, on fifteen skills that belong to the night and are gone when it ends. They are not the Skills on the title screen (those are bought with levels and kept): three branches of five, each skill under the one it needs. Reaper: Steady Hands (10% less recoil a rank), Quick Trigger (+6% rate of fire), Bone Shatter (a head-shot kill bursts for 60 within 3 m), Spoils (a head-shot kill puts a round back in the magazine), Killing Spree (each kill within 3 seconds of the last adds 4% damage, up to 40%). Revenant: Soul Leech (a kill restores 1 health), Spirit Armor (+20 armor when a wave begins), Backlash (being hit hurts and throws back the dead within 3 m), Death's Door (20% less damage below a third of your health), Borrowed Time (once a wave, the blow that would put you down is turned aside). Lightkeeper: Long Reach (wisps come from 3 m further), Rich Harvest (+15% lifeforce), Tithe ($3 for every point gathered), Warding Light (the crystal takes 10% less), Soul Nova (every 40 lifeforce gathered bursts from you for up to 220 within 8 m). A first rank costs 6 to 8, the last skill of a branch 150, the whole tree 1,340. Under the tree, lifeforce can be traded for money: $10 a point, ten at a time or all of it. Playing together, each player sees and gathers their own.

**Recoil has a shape, aimed or not.** A burst from an automatic climbs hard for three rounds, walks right to the tenth, left to the eighteenth, then sways, the same every time, so it can be learned and held against. A weapon fired a round at a time steps left and right in a fixed order of six. Aiming no longer takes the kick away: an aimed carbine climbs about half a degree a round (it was a quarter) and the muzzle is seen to rise in the sights.

**The gun in hand.** The lower right of the screen shows the weapon as a large silhouette with its name, the rounds left in large figures and a bar of how full that is; on the Churchyard a row of chips under it counts each kind of ammunition, the one in use lit. The silhouettes are the game's own models drawn side on and redrawn as icons by an image generator (`tools/hud-icons/build.mjs`, `CREDITS.md`).

**Recoil.** A shot throws the view up and a little aside, and part of that stays: about half a degree a round from a carbine at the hip for the first nine rounds of a burst, less after, so a magazine emptied without correcting climbs about nine degrees. Heavier weapons kick harder (the shotgun and the heavy pistol about twice as much, the PDW about half), aiming takes a fifth off and crouching a little more, and the gunsmith's compensator and grips take off what they say. On the Churchyard the Soldier's Adrenaline halves it, the armory sells a Recoil Harness (30% less), Steady Aim on the title's Skills takes 4% a rank and Steady Hands on the Lifeforce tab 10% a rank.

**Prices (Churchyard).** Everything the armory sells for money costs a quarter more than it did (a guard $1,130, a turret $1,500, a grenade $150), and the flak cannon and the rocket launcher cost double.

**Announcements.** A level gained, a challenge finished and an outfit earned are announced at top centre, one at a time. A level-up comes down from above the screen and lands: a dark plate between two gold rules, the rank's badge slammed onto it with rings and sparks, the level rolling from the old number to the new, a gold flash across the top of the screen and a line of light at the banner's height, and a fanfare (a drum, four rising notes of brass, a held chord under a cymbal; one voice more for a promotion). The others drop in as a gold title over a line of text. Experience gained is listed beside the reticle (a head shot says so). Nothing is announced behind the pause screen; it waits. With the system's "reduce motion" setting on, announcements fade in and out and nothing else moves.

**Leaderboards.** Every mode has its own: the fastest extraction in Terminal Breach; the highest score and the most waves in Holdout; the longest vigil, the most zombies in one vigil and the longest vigil with a squad in Crystal Vigil; and across both maps, machines and zombies destroyed and outfits earned. A run goes to its boards while it is being played, not only when it ends: when a wave is cleared, when you pause, when the tab is hidden, and every twenty seconds of play in between, so a night that is never lost (or a tab that is closed) still ranks. A line at the top of the screen says so when a wave moves you up a board. The card at the end of a run shows where it ranked, and the title screen shows the top three of the map you are on. Boards need the Multiplayer Agent key; without it the screen says so. `?noboards=1` in the address plays without writing to them.

**Adding a mode.** A new mode reports what happens in it (`GunslingerAchievements.report(fact, details)`), defines its challenges in a file like `src/challenges.js`, and declares its boards (`GunslingerBoards.define`); counting, saving, the Operator screen, the end card and the Leaderboards screen pick them up.

## Visual & Rendering Architecture

- **Neutral Photographic Grade & Live Optical Sync**: `vendor/gunslinger-engine.js` syncs the `g.render.tune` optical uniforms (`aoRadius`, `aoIntensity`, `aoStrength`, `volSigma`, `volMaxDist`, `volFarHaze`, `bloomMix`, `lookPower`, `lookSat`, `uSplit`, `uBlackLift`) every frame. The default grade is neutral (no teal/orange split-tone, deep neutral blacks, restrained bloom and flare, lighter aerial haze so mid-ground construction keeps contrast).
- **Two-Bounce Geometry-Aware Harbor IBL**: `src/visuals.js` captures a 2-bounce 256 px HalfFloat HDR `CubeCamera` → `PMREMGenerator` environment probe in the container yard plus the transit-shed interior probe so wet concrete, painted steel, and weapon metals reflect surrounding harbor structures rather than unoccluded sky.
- **2048 px Procedural Materials & Derived Concrete Surface Relief**: Unlocks 2048 px procedural harbor textures with 16× anisotropic filtering, and derives a pixel-registered tangent-slope normal, micro-roughness, and pore-cavity map on CPU/Canvas from `assets/materials/dock-concrete-albedo.png`. Luminance is pre-filtered before the slope pass and the shader widens roughness where slopes vary faster than a pixel (specular anti-aliasing), so the damp apron does not glitter. Damp (non-puddle) concrete samples the planar reflection as a lower-blur, vertically stretched streak rather than an isotropic high-mip blob.
- **Planar Harbor Water Reflections & Distance Wave Filtering**: Binds the oblique-clipped planar reflection pass into the `sea` shader with `-1.6 m` sea-plane parallax correction, camera-aligned vertical wave-column stretching, hoisted quay/hull foam masking, and distance capillary-slope filtering to eliminate mid-range water Moiré shimmer.
- **Texel-Snapped High-Density Shadows & Practical Harbor Lights**: Focuses the 4096 directional shadow frustum tightly around the active play area (`~1.2 cm/texel`) quantized in light-space texel increments, updates shadows every frame on `High` and `Ultra`, and drives a culled pool of 6 practical point lights harvested from harbor floodlight masts, transit-shed fixtures, and the site office.
- **Calibrated Viewmodel & Lit PBR Decals**: Hardcoat anodized aluminium is treated as a dark dielectric oxide over metal (moderate metalness) rather than a black mirror, with more metallic phosphate/nitride steels and a realistic ~4 % black-polymer albedo, so machined form reads at hip and in the gunsmith. Gloves are ranger-green synthetic backs with coyote suede palms; sleeves are a charcoal-olive softshell with subdued panels (the hi-vis band and reflective stripe are gone), and outdoor rain wetness on gun and arms is moderated to match the light rain. The default rifle and pistol hip poses carry less yaw and cant so the muzzle tracks the crosshair. A 3-point viewmodel light rig, per-vertex AO on Hound/Gull robots, supersonic projectile tracers, and a bounded 96-decal pool of normal-mapped `MeshStandardMaterial` bullet craters complete the layer.
- **Contextual HUD**: the weapon strip appears only when the selection changes, the control primer fades after the opening seconds of an operation, and in-HUD branding and the redundant stance readout are removed; objective, compass, health, endurance/breath and the weapon readiness line stay.

The Churchyard brings its own night look, lighting and ground (`src/vigil/`), and skips the passes above that belong to the terminal: the apron, the sea, the harbor yard IBL probe, the practical mast lights and the robots' AO. It takes the focused, texel-snapped shadow fit with its own numbers: a 64 m box ahead of the camera (about 1.6 cm a texel, against 4.7 cm for the engine's fit over the whole 136 m square), widening to the whole yard for views from above. Its nave reflection probe is captured down the nave (`interiorProbe` in `src/vigil/level.js`) rather than at the centre of the church, where the crystal stands and turned every interior surface cyan. The weapon's fill lights are night ones: a dim cool key, a moonlit rim and almost no bounce, where the Harbor's daylight rig had lit the gun as brightly as the moon. The viewmodel calibration, tracers and impact decals apply on both maps.

### How a map plugs into the engine

A map is an object in `window.GunslingerMaps`. If it sets `engine: true`, `src/maps.js` makes it `window.GunslingerMap` before the engine script is parsed, and the engine consults it at ten places (an eleventh addition, `game.title()`, belongs to no map: it returns to the title screen from the pause and end cards):

1. `configure(internals)` runs once, before boot, with the engine's own tables: world bounds, sun and sky colours, texture and material definitions, the sound table, key bindings, the rain factory, holdout tuning, and `setNav` to resize the navigation grid.
2. `createLevel`, `createBackdrop`, `createGround` and `createEnemies` replace the harbor's builders.
3. `skyFragment()` and `horizonGLSL` replace the sky shader and the horizon colour the fog uses.
4. `shadowBounds` sets the area the shadow map covers.
5. `bootText` renames the loading messages.
6. A level may return `interiorProbe` (a position) to place the interior reflection probe; by default it is the centre of the first interior box.

The gameplay and visual layers read a few more fields: `arsenal` (`false`, or a plan for running the arsenal under the map's rules, documented at the top of the arsenal section of `src/gunslinger.js`), `shadowFocus` and `viewLights` (`src/visuals.js`), `look(g)` and `install(g)`.

With no map selected none of these run and the engine behaves exactly as before. `tests/vigil.test.cjs` checks both cases.

### How co-op works

There is no game server. A Multiplayer Agent room relays state and events between the browsers in it, and one of them, the host, runs the game for the rest:

- **Each player** writes only their own entry in the room: position, aim, health, class, a few flags. The others draw a watchman's body there, with a rifle and a name.
- **The host** thinks for the dead, counts the waves, keeps the crystal and aims the guards and turrets. Ten times a second it writes every zombie on its feet into one string of room state, twelve characters each (id, kind, position to 3 cm, heading, limbs, health); four times a second the wave and crystal state; twice a second what has been placed and what is broken.
- **Everyone else** slides each zombie toward where the host last put it and animates it locally, so limbs, crawling and ragdolls look the same without being sent. Their shots are tested against those bodies on their own machine and sent to the host as hits; the host applies them and announces deaths, with who made the kill and what push the body took.
- **Things that break** are cut from a seed the host sends, so every machine breaks them along the same planes and their colliders match.
- **If the host leaves** or stops answering for three and a half seconds, the remaining player with the lowest session id takes over from what it was last shown: the dead keep their positions, health and missing limbs, and the spawn queue is rebuilt from the count the old host last gave.

This trusts the players' machines, which suits a co-op game against the dead and would not suit a competitive one.

To host a copy: put your own key in `mp.config.local.js` (`window.MP_CONFIG.key = 'pk_live_…';`), run `node tools/build-host.mjs` (it wants `sharp` for the textures), and publish the files in `dist/` to the game with Multiplayer Agent's `publish_game` (large files go through `create_upload` first; `dist/files.json` lists every path with its size).

## How the bodies are painted

The dead and the watchmen are meshes built in code (`src/vigil/figure.js`) and painted from generated artwork. `tools/zombie-art` does it in three steps:

1. `mannequin.cjs` draws each body as a plain grey figure on a magenta background: whole body and head, front and back, in the pose the mesh is painted in.
2. `generate.mjs` hands those pictures to an image generator with the descriptions in `prompts.cjs` and asks it to paint the character over the grey figure without moving anything. The pictures in `tools/zombie-art/art` are what came back; `concept/` holds the two concept sheets the designs were settled on first.
3. `bake.cjs` keys out the magenta, projects the front and back pictures onto the posed mesh (the head from its own close-ups), wraps them a little way round the sides, cuts tattered hems from what was painted, and writes one 2048 × 1024 atlas per body into `assets/zombies`.

Steps 1 and 3 need Node and the `sharp` package (`npm install --no-save sharp`). Step 2 uses the image tool of a signed-in Codex CLI; any image generator that can paint over a reference picture would do. If a body's shape changes in `figure.js`, repeat all three steps for that body, because the artwork is fitted to the mesh.

## Provenance and Remaining Limits

The foundational prototype credits Felix for improvements to a Claude-generated game. `assets/materials/dock-concrete-albedo.png` is a locally generated base-color texture from which `src/visuals.js` derives matching normal/roughness/cavity maps at load time; it is not a multi-capture photogrammetry scan.

Benchmark on Apple M5 (`?bench=1&seed=4207&q=high`, 3234×1838 buffer at DPR 2, single run each): frame time avg 17.7 ms / median 15.7 ms / p99 25.3 ms after the South Stack Yard and viewmodel changes, versus 16.9 / 15.0 / 25.1 ms before; the larger map adds roughly 1 M triangles in the benchmark view. Lighting, reflections, material response and the first-person weapon are substantially upgraded, but world architecture and robot enemies remain procedural geometry rather than sculpted/scanned meshes, the glove and sleeve are procedural shader detail on a generated arm mesh (not scanned hands), same-platform weapon swaps use spring-driven draw motion rather than bespoke two-hand swap choreography, and world-cover bullet penetration/ricochet and gamepad/touch parity for gunsmith controls remain future work.

Crystal Vigil's rules (classes, waves, prices, zombie types, defenses) come from the Crystal Vigil prototype. Its level and models were rebuilt for this engine rather than copied: the church, graveyard, forest, zombies, guards and turrets here are new geometry. Severed limbs, crawling, ragdolls, breakable objects, stacked waves and co-op are additions; the prototype had none of them.

Limits to know about:

- The churchyard downloads about 20 MB of textures; the harbor downloads one.
- The dead and the watchmen are meshes built in code and painted from generated artwork, not sculpted or scanned characters. The paint is projected from a front and a back picture, so detail is softer on the sides of a body than on its front. There is no facial animation beyond the jaw.
- Ragdolls and broken pieces collide with the ground and the level's colliders, not with each other, the player or the living dead. Pieces of a broken object that come to rest become scenery: you can walk through them.
- Stumps are the only thing left standing of a broken stone; a stump cannot be broken further.
- The churchyard is flat where you can walk; the engine's physics is boxes on a level floor.
- Only a few real lights exist at once. They follow the flames nearest the camera, so a distant lantern shows its glow but lights nothing until you approach.
- A zombie's way into the church is chosen when it appears and changed only at a door it passes. It does not know where your guards and turrets stand. The dead do not climb the graveyard wall or come through windows.
- The soldiers are War Bash's: built in code, with every face covered. They are lit, shadowed and weathered like the rest of the scene, but their shapes are simpler than the maps around them: hats, fur and some plates are faceted, the camouflage is large flat shapes, hands are fixed in two grips. They move by War Bash's procedural pose. Its motion-captured cycles look better, most of all at a run (a forward lean, a longer stride), but the only baked copy is the original author's, which is not under the MIT License: bake your own from a Mixamo account and `node tools/soldier/import.mjs <War Bash> --anim` takes them in (see `CREDITS.md`).
- Other players and the hired Rifleman Guards are soldiers (the guards in the Commando outfit); your own first-person arms do not change with your outfit. The painted watchman's body remains only as the fallback when the soldier code is not loaded.
- The first soldier of a session takes a moment to appear (about half a second to build an outfit, then its shader): the worn outfit is prepared on the title screen, a teammate's is built when they join.
- Challenges and unlocks are counted on your machine and saved as you report them: someone who edits their save can wear anything. Leaderboard names are a callsign made from the player id until a player has a claimed name.
- The Harbor has no network play.
- Co-op runs on one player's machine. If that browser tab is hidden its game stops and the room waits about four seconds before another player takes over. Hits are decided on the shooter's machine, so they land where you aimed even with lag, and a cheating client would be believed. Other players do not block shots, and nobody can be revived by a teammate: going down works as it does alone.
- Pieces thrown from a broken object fly differently on each machine; where the object was cut, and its collider afterwards, are the same.
